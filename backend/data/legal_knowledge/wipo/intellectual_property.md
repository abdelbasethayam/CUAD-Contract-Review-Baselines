WIPO describes technology transfer agreements as formal contracts used to
transfer technology, knowledge, and information. WIPO states that there is no
single standard agreement for all technology transfers and that model contracts
are starting points that should be adapted to the specific circumstances of
each transaction.

For licensing, WIPO explains that a licensor gives a licensee permission to use
intellectual property in ways stated in the agreement. The license terms define
the licensee's rights, including whether the permission is broad or narrow by
purpose, territory, field of use, distribution, manufacturing, duration, and
other negotiated limits. WIPO also identifies software licensing as one of the
licensing contexts addressed by technology transfer model agreements.

For assignments, WIPO explains that an assignment transfers ownership of
intellectual property from the owner to the assignee with permanent effect. The
assignment contract must accurately identify the subject matter being assigned,
such as patents, patent applications, copyright, trademarks, utility models,
know-how, or trade secrets.

WIPO distinguishes licensing from assignment: a license allows use of IP under
negotiated conditions and may leave ownership with the licensor, while an
assignment permanently divests the former owner of ownership. For collaborative
research, WIPO identifies ownership of intellectual property rights, access
rights, benefit and risk sharing, and commercialization rights as part of the
legal framework that parties define.

For confidentiality, WIPO describes non-disclosure and confidentiality
agreements as contracts that restrict disclosure or use of confidential
information. WIPO notes that effective confidentiality terms should define what
information is confidential, who may access it, what measures are required to
keep it confidential, and a reasonable period for the obligations.

This system uses WIPO's guidance only as supporting context for potential
review indicators. It does not treat an IP license, assignment, ownership term,
or confidentiality term as inherently risky.
