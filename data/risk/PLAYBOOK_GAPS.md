# Phase 2 playbook coverage and gaps

The current commercial risk playbook is an issue-spotting policy, not a complete commercial-contract control library.

## Current coverage

The imported team checklist remains the authoritative internal policy baseline. CUAD categories remain useful for clause detection and segmentation, but CUAD presence is not treated as a risk conclusion.

## Recommended additional families

These are tracked as **coverage candidates**, not silently converted into risk rules until the team supplies an approved policy question, source basis, and gold examples:

- Parties / authority / signatory authority
- Definitions and interpretation
- Effective date / term / expiration
- Delivery / acceptance / inspection
- Service levels / credits / service remedies
- Data protection / privacy
- Security / incident response / AI-use terms
- Compliance / anti-bribery / sanctions / export controls
- Breach / notice / cure
- IP infringement procedure
- Business continuity / disaster recovery
- Records retention / audit remediation
- Subcontracting / flow-down
- Notices / amendment / waiver
- Severability / entire agreement / counterparts / e-signature
- Publicity / use of names
- Insolvency
- Survival
- Escrow / source-code continuity
- Third-party beneficiary
- ROFR / ROFO / ROFN
- MFN
- Revenue / profit sharing
- Price restrictions
- Volume restrictions
- Non-disparagement
- Covenant not to sue

## Do not generalize across contract types

A rule should declare its intended contract type and perspective. Sales-law authorities, employment restrictions, competition guidance, real-estate provisions, and technology licenses must not be generalized to other agreements without an explicit applicability decision.

## Required promotion gate

A candidate family becomes a production playbook rule only when it has:

1. Atomic review questions.
2. Applicability conditions.
3. Positive and negative examples.
4. Evidence requirements.
5. Source tier and jurisdiction scope.
6. Approved owner/version/effective date.
7. Human-adjudicated gold examples.
8. Locked-test evaluation coverage.
